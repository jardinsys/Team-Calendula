## Working draft (v7 — continuity framing for Systemiser, disorder-specific roadmap)

> More than one billion people worldwide live with a mental health condition, yet support and treatment reach only a fraction of those affected, making mental health one of the most under-resourced areas of modern health care.
> Within that population, only some conditions attract significant research funding and technology investment while others remain largely invisible, drawing disproportionately little research support relative to their real-world burden.
> At the same time, today's rapid advances in software platforms, real-time collaboration, AI, and privacy engineering make it newly feasible to build assistive technology for mental health, which makes the gap between what technology could do and what it actually serves more urgent rather than less.
> My solution, Team Calendula, is an independent software project created to narrow this gap: it develops technology for conditions that receive little mainstream attention, with its first focus being dissociative disorders.
> Its first product, Systemiser, supports this goal: designed around the unifying need for continuity when dissociative symptoms disrupt memory, identity, and the organization of daily life, it lets users represent the distinct identity states that characterize these conditions, track transitions between them, maintain a structured record of events, status, and functioning that bridges memory gaps, and share those details selectively through a granular privacy model.
> Systemiser is being built as an application ecosystem spanning desktop, mobile, and web, with a real-time collaboration layer and privacy-focused cloud-hosted data storage.
> It also plans gateways into third-party platform categories, including messaging and collaboration platforms such as Discord and Slack, and in-vehicle assistant platforms such as Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, serving as an example of how platforms that already shape daily digital life can be turned toward assistive purposes.
> Prototype testing has exercised four core feature cycles to date: identity-state representation and transitions, status and functioning tracking, privacy-gated sharing, and collaborative information management, with additional cycles under active development to address disorder-specific needs, including grounding techniques and crisis-support tools.
> I present Systemiser as the first case study in Team Calendula's broader trajectory: a growing family of products that address more under-represented conditions over time, closing the mental-health technology gap condition by condition.
